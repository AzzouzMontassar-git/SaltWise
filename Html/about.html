About SaltWise & SUSTAIN

Empowering global innovation for salt-affected lands

SaltWise is an innovative digital platform developed as part of the SUSTAIN COST Action (CA22144) – a European Research Network dedicated to the sustainable use of salt-affected lands. Salinisation, the accumulation of water-soluble salts in the soil, is one of the major causes of soil degradation affecting 833 million hectares of land worldwide. SUSTAIN unites over 200 experts from 35+ countries to build a global transdisciplinary network, advancing knowledge and practices for reclaiming and managing saline soils. As a key tool within this network, SaltWise provides accessible resources, diagnostics, and community forums to empower farmers, researchers, and policymakers in turning degradation into innovation.

Chadha Ghediri

Lead Developer & Soil Scientist. With expertise in agroecological innovations, Chadha drives SaltWise's development, focusing on practical tools for saline soil rehabilitation. As a key contributor to SUSTAIN's WG3 (Upscaling Sustainable Management Practices), Chadha ensures the platform aligns with cutting-edge research for real-world impact across Europe and beyond.

The SUSTAIN Team

A collaborative network led by WG3 (upscaling practices) and WG6 (knowledge transfer). Including leaders like Zenep (WG3) and Nour (WG6), the team integrates SaltWise with Deliverable D3.1 – the Best Practices Database – to bridge lab findings with field applications, fostering resilient agriculture under the EU Green Deal.

SaltWise Mission

At the heart of SUSTAIN's mission to combat soil degradation, SaltWise democratizes saline agriculture solutions. By linking user diagnostics to WG3's best practices and fostering community discussions, it promotes resilient farming that restores soil health, boosts productivity, and supports climate adaptation for food and water security.

Join us in SUSTAIN's effort to transform salt challenges into opportunities for sustainable agriculture. Explore our resources, contribute to the network, and help shape the future of salt-affected lands.
